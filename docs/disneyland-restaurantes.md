# Disneyland food recommendations (Sarah's favorite spots)

Here's where to eat at Disneyland. These are all Sarah's picks, and we've been enough times that she's got opinions about it.

Breakfast, just eat the buffet at whatever hotel you're staying at. She never bothers with anything fancier in the morning. The character meals are there if you want one, Goofy's Kitchen at the Disneyland Hotel and Storytellers Cafe over at the Grand Californian, and they're not only a breakfast thing. Goofy's does the same buffet with the characters at dinner. Storytellers serves dinner too, but the characters only come around in the morning. Sarah's take is that they're really built for families with kids, so skip it if that's not you.

Lunch, don't plan it at all. That's the one thing she tells everybody. Eat wherever you happen to be standing in the park, because walking all the way across Disneyland for a reservation wrecks your afternoon. The Star Wars area is the exception. It's her favorite part of the park and she'd make you go over there hungry or not. The food is strange on purpose and the whole place looks like the movie. Oga's Cantina is in there too, a bar with a DJ droid and some very odd drinks, and that one you book separately.

If you want a real sit-down meal without leaving the park, Blue Bayou is her favorite. You eat inside the Pirates of the Caribbean ride, in the dark, right next to the water, with the boats drifting past. Book it the day reservations open or you won't get in. Café Orleans is right around the corner, cheaper and more relaxed, and she likes it almost as much. Carnation Cafe on Main Street if you just want normal American food.

Dinner she'd send you out to Downtown Disney, which doesn't need a park ticket. Din Tai Fung is the one she always wants, Taiwanese dumplings, but get your name in early because the wait gets long. Naples is the Italian place and it's never a bad call. She also really likes Paseo and Céntrico, same chef, same building: Paseo upstairs for a proper dinner, Céntrico downstairs for drinks and small plates. They're on the side of Downtown Disney that backs onto the Grand Californian. And if you do want one night with the characters, Goofy's Kitchen is easier to book at dinner than at breakfast.

Then Trader Sam's, the tiki bar at the Disneyland Hotel, which is how she likes to end the night. Order a drink and the whole room does something. She'd tell you it's the most fun in the entire resort.

Anyway, that's the list.
