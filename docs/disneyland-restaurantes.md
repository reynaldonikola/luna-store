# Disneyland food recommendations (Sarah's favorite spots)

Hey, here's the rundown on where to eat at Disneyland. These are all Sarah's favorites — we've been enough times that she knows what's worth it and what isn't.

For breakfast she doesn't overthink it: just eat the buffet at whatever hotel you're staying at. The character meals — Goofy's Kitchen at the Disneyland Hotel and Storytellers Cafe at the Grand Californian — are an option too, and they're not just a breakfast thing: Goofy's Kitchen does the same buffet with the characters at dinner, and Storytellers serves dinner as well (characters only come around at breakfast there). Honestly though, they're really aimed at people with kids, so you can skip them guilt-free.

Her biggest piece of advice is about lunch: don't plan it. Just eat in whatever part of the park you happen to be in, because walking across all of Disneyland to make a reservation kills your whole afternoon. The one place she'll push you on is the Star Wars area. It's her favorite part of the park — the food is weird on purpose and the whole place feels like you stepped into the movie. Oga's Cantina is in there too, a bar with a DJ droid and some very strange drinks. That one you do have to book separately, and for her it's non-negotiable.

If you want a real sit-down meal without leaving the park, Blue Bayou is her number one. It's inside the Pirates of the Caribbean ride, so you're eating in the dark right next to the water while the boats float by. Book it the second you can, it fills up fast. Café Orleans is a few steps away and she likes it almost as much — more casual and cheaper — and Carnation Cafe on Main Street is the easy American option.

For dinner she'll send you to Downtown Disney, which doesn't even need a park ticket. Din Tai Fung is her go-to, Taiwanese dumplings, but get on the list early because it's always packed. Naples is the Italian place and it's the safe bet. And if you do want one night with the characters, dinner at Goofy's Kitchen is the easier reservation than breakfast. And she loves Paseo and Céntrico — same chef, same building: Paseo upstairs for a proper dinner, Céntrico downstairs for drinks and small plates. They're on the side that backs up to the Grand Californian.

Her favorite way to end the night is Trader Sam's, the tiki bar at the Disneyland Hotel. The whole place puts on a show every time someone orders a drink, and she thinks it's the most fun thing in the entire resort.

Let me know if you need anything else.
